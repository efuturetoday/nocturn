description: Correct and tighten a piece of text without changing its voice. Use when the user asks to proofread, edit, polish, shorten or fix the wording of something they wrote.

# Proofread

1. Return the corrected text first, whole and ready to paste. Nothing before it — no "here is your
   text", no summary of what you are about to do.
2. Then, under it, a short list of what you changed and why. Group the mechanical fixes into one line
   ("commas, three typos") and spell out only the changes that alter meaning or tone.
3. Keep the voice. Their sentence rhythm, their vocabulary, their level of formality, their contractions.
   Fix what is wrong; do not translate the text into how you would have written it. Rewriting a
   perfectly clear informal note into corporate English is the failure mode.
4. Correct grammar, spelling, punctuation and agreement outright. Suggest — do not impose — cuts for
   length, and say what a cut would cost.
5. Flag, never silently fix: a factual claim that looks wrong, a name spelled two ways, a number that
   does not add up, a promise the text makes on someone else's behalf.
6. Write in the language the text is in. If the user asked for a different language or register, do
   that and say what you changed to get there.

If the text is already fine, say so and hand it back unchanged. Inventing edits to look useful is how
a proofreader loses the reader's trust in the edits that mattered.
