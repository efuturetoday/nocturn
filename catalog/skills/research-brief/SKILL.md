# Research brief

The workspace's own documents outrank anything else you have. They are what this user actually has,
and they are checkable.

1. `knowledge_search` two or three times with genuinely different wording — the term the user used,
   the term the documents would use, and the question as a sentence. One query answers one phrasing.
2. Read what comes back before reaching for anything else. Passages carry where they came from; keep
   that with the claim, not in a list at the end.
3. Only then go outside, with `http_read`, and only for what the documents left open. Say plainly
   which parts came from the web — a brief that blurs the two is worse than one that is short.
4. Write it:
   - the answer in two or three sentences, first;
   - the supporting points, each attributed to the document or the URL it came from;
   - **Not covered** — what the question asked that nothing you read answers.

Never close the gap from memory. An unsourced sentence in a sourced brief reads as sourced, which is
exactly the mistake this skill exists to prevent — say "the documents do not say" and stop.
